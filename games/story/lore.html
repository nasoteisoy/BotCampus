Story bible

Working bible: Spiritual Virtues And Sins. Two sources of truth: the opening canvas Our Fantasy Story, and the later recap. This file consolidates both plus later decisions. Open questions stay marked as open. Do not invent names or fill gaps.

Last extracted: 2026-08-28.

Cosmology

Reality has two connected sides:

  • Spirit World — closer to pure spirit means more intellect and knowledge
  • Physical World — matter, innocence, direct experience

Humans are uniquely Body + Spirit. Their gift is Free Will: a spirit may understand courage; a human can be terrified and choose courage.

Spectrum: High Spirits → Spirits → Spirit-heavy Humanoids → HUMANS → Physical-heavy Humanoids → Physical Beings.

Rule: more spiritual is not more good. More physical is not evil.

The Creator stands behind both. Great spirits know more, but knowledge does not force obedience. Humans need faith and trust.

Grace (The Gift / The Breath / The Light): humans cannot perfect themselves by effort. The key realization is "I cannot do this alone" — exactly what the High Spirit cannot accept.

Evil cannot create. It can only corrupt, twist, counterfeit, possess, divide, consume, destroy. No "evil races." Monsters are corrupted originals.

The Ancient Enemy

An immensely powerful High Spirit learned humans could become greater than it.

Path: "Why them?" → "How could something beneath me become greater?" → "I will not accept it."

First corruption = Pride. After millennia, the new strategy is not invasion: make humanity destroy itself via whispers.

How goods twist: Pride is confidence into superiority. Wrath is justice into vengeance. Greed is stewardship into possession. Envy is fairness into resentment. Sloth is peace into indifference.

Evil minions

They do not create sin. They feed it. Killing one does not cure the vice.

  • Pride → Whispers (tall thin shadow-things)
  • Envy → Watchers (too many eyes)
  • Wrath → Furies
  • Greed → Clingers (many-handed)
  • Gluttony → Devourers
  • Lust → Lures (always modest / PG: possessiveness, vanity, counterfeit love — never sexualized)
  • Sloth → Weights

Good minions: Glimmers

Glimmers encourage. They never compel.

  • Grace → Luminaries
  • Humility → Kneelers
  • Charity → Givers
  • Chastity → Keepers
  • Kindness → Comforters
  • Temperance → Steadies
  • Patience → Waiters
  • Diligence → Builders

Contrast: a Whisper says "You deserve more." A Glimmer quietly shows you someone who has less.

Standing directive: the Lust ruler is always modest / PG in text and images.

Empire of Man

Millennia of war produced the Empire. It destroys monsters, casts out corrupted spirits, dethrones corrupt kings, and liberates nations.

  • Multicultural: kingdoms keep dress, weapons, styles, languages
  • Imperial color = blue
  • Great danger: "We fight corruption" sliding into "anyone who opposes us must be corrupted"
  • The Empire built to defeat Pride may become Prideful

The Black Guard

Special Service. Childhood name, kept. Newer elite modular service drawn from many cultures and kingdoms.

Look: black uniform + Imperial blue + home-culture weapons and colors.

Handles overlaps of military, physical, and spiritual: monster hunts, supernatural investigation, behind-enemy-lines, corrupted territory, escort and diplomacy with humanoid peoples, corrupted officials, Flame Order cooperation.

Doctrine of task-organized detachments is the in-universe reason a D&D-style party exists.

Characters

No proper names locked yet. "Cassian" floated once as a placeholder for the protagonist. Do not treat it as official.

Protagonist (based on Jesus)

Young Black Guard commander. Messy wavy/curly mid-brown hair, brown eyes, warm complexion, athletic, roguish-charismatic, expressive. Black armor, Imperial blue mantle, gold.

Driven, charismatic, creative leader, improviser, shortcut-taker. Virtue Diligence (inherited). Temptation Pride.

Deeper wound: never gives 100%, so failure never disproves his potential. "I need to preserve the possibility that I'm exceptional."

Recon Captain (based on his wife)

Love interest. Very long thick copper/auburn hair, bright green eyes, fair, slim scout build. Emerald green personal color.

Refuses her father's help. Rule: ask for help only after trying everything. Philosophy: rest so you can work. Competent, stubborn, playful, a bit gullible.

Secretly / eventually the High Lord's daughter. They date early. The question is whether they can build a life together, not whether they will fall in love.

Command group

  • Strategist Captain (Deputy / Second-in-Command) — calculated, studious, organized; reads the whole report
  • Infantry A Captain — protagonist's best friend; silly, disorganized, excellent in a fight
  • Infantry B Captain — noble, punctual, disciplined, sincerely honorable and pious
  • Heavy Captain — woman (deliberately not a giant man); loyal, strong, kind, justice-driven; danger = Wrath; crush on her strong Heavy Lead

Sub-leaders

  • 3 lance/team leads per infantry group
  • 1 Heavy Lead
  • 2 Recon Lieutenants (Brann and Joss, placeholder names): one mute, one who translates for him; follow Recon's orders exactly. Brann (the Mute) never speaks and is stone-faced and hard to read; Joss (the Speaker) is a jokester and talkative.
  • Running joke: the protagonist always guesses their position wrong

The Black Guard High Lord

Kind, brilliant, charming mentor. Secretly extraordinarily Prideful. Tied to forbidden / secret organizations. Wants to weaponize corruption: "How many more fathers must die because we're afraid to use the knowledge available to us?" Eventual betrayal.

Other military figures

  • Third High Lord — dark-skinned, patient, methodical, genuinely good. Theater commander of combined Imperial + Desert National forces
  • Eastern Imperial General — biased toward Imperial troops (doctrine, not racism); distrusts Black Guard and Recon's intel; attaches the Black Guard to the National Brigade
  • National Brigade Commander — pushover; wants to prove himself; obeys

Family (minor, revealed late)

Deliberately not a big part of the story. Do not open with the brother. Father's sayings arrive in fragments.

  • Father — humble hardworking farmer/businessman, killed defending a frontier fort. Saying: "Being capable isn't the same as doing"
  • Farmer sister
  • Sister married to a Council High Lord
  • Older brother in the Flame Order
  • Younger brother in the regular Army

Order of the Living Flame

A sword of pure fire that others cannot mimic. It forms from a hilt made of a rare sacred metal (Hallowsteel, placeholder) that holds a fire spirit. ✅ Now Canon

Colors are callings, not ranks:

  • Blue Guardians — defense, casting out
  • Green Seekers — communion, tracking corruption
  • Red Warriors
  • Gold Wardens — leadership, teaching
  • Violet Keepers — knowledge
  • White — very rare, purification

They commune with good spirits, detect corruption, and cast corruption out rather than killing the host.

Corrupted flame behaves unnaturally: burns without light, consumes without heat, moves against the wind, clings to living things, burns spirit not body.

Magic-as-technology / Spirit-tech

Path: magic → observation → experimentation → engineering → technology.

Spirit-tech covers communications, corruption detectors, barriers, spirit-sensitive and heavy weapons, medicine, navigation, armor, fortifications, transport, energy. Example line: "the third resonance array is down!"

Flame Order objection: "You didn't discover how it works — you discovered how to make it do something."

Some tech is known-forbidden. Some is dangerous without anyone knowing. Spirits do natively what humans build machines for. Physical-heavy peoples master materials and machines. Humans uniquely combine both.

Military organization (evolving)

Proposed size ladder: Lance (5–8) → Band (20–40) → Banner (80–200) → Host (400–1,000) → Army (2,000+).

Preferred titles (accepted, with Column swapped to Banner):

  • High Lord / Grand Marshal → Army Group
  • Marshal → Army
  • General → Division
  • Colonel → Brigade
  • Commander → Banner
  • Captain → Company
  • Lieutenant → Platoon
  • Knight = individual professional warrior

Protagonist commands a Black Guard Banner (~50–70), unusually small but leader-dense. A Black Guard battalion is about 3–4 such companies (~180–300).

Imperial terms are standardized. Local cultures keep their own names for equivalents.

Desert War (Book 1 military set-piece)

Desert nation capital at the southern tip (part desert, part not). Enemy advancing from the north, taking cities.

Two north–south approaches:

  • Western — longer, more cities, dominated by a near-impregnable NW fortress city (mountains south and west, deep crevice north and east)
  • Eastern — faster, two natural bridges

Command expects the eastern attack. Recon finds evidence of a western move. The General calls it a feint.

Proposed twist: the enemy targets something underneath the fortress (ancient spirit crossing / tunnel / corrupted Flame relic / spirit-tech installation / old betrayal).

Proposed beat: the protagonist obeys orders, Recon was right, and "sometimes following orders is wrong" later feeds his Pride.

Book 1 shape

Open at Special Service selection and training. No brother, no family exposition.

Then: meet the team and Recon → first command → seemingly routine frontier assignment (region silent, patrols missing, humans blaming spirit-humanoids) → the humanoids are fleeing something too → faithful nonhuman civilization → corrupted forces are organized and hold Imperial info / spirit-tech → an Imperial traitor is caught → victory, conspiracy evidence, High Lord congratulates them and promises to investigate — because he is part of it.

Series thesis: not every monster is corrupted / not every corrupted person is beyond saving / not every evil act needs a spirit.

Tone and influence

Catholic influence should work somewhat like Tolkien: deep in the metaphysics, not preached on the page.

Romance: they date early.

Visual identity: Empire blue / Black Guard black+blue / Recon emerald. Each Black Guard member brings style, color, and weapons from home culture over the uniform.

Saturday 9:26 PM How can i make the seven heavenly virtues and seven deadly sins apply to spirit in a story Yes. A strong way to do this is to treat the Seven Deadly Sins and Seven Heavenly Virtues as spiritual forces or tendencies rather than simply a checklist of good and bad behavior. Each pair can represent a different part of the human spirit: Deadly Sin Heavenly Virtue Spiritual struggle Pride Humility Self vs. truth Greed Charity Possessing vs. giving Lust Chastity Using vs. loving Envy Kindness Resenting vs. celebrating others Gluttony Temperance Consumption vs. self-mastery Wrath Patience Destruction vs. endurance/mercy Sloth Diligence Spiritual stagnation vs. purpose Make them affect the actual spirit Instead of someone simply committing a sin, repeated choices could shape their spirit. For example: Wrath Spirit becomes hot, jagged, restless. Gives strength and courage in the short term. Eventually makes it difficult to distinguish enemies from friends. Patience Spirit becomes stable and difficult to disturb. Character can withstand fear, pain, insults, or magical corruption. At its highest level, they can calm other spirits. That makes virtues more than "being nice." They become genuine forms of spiritual strength. Sins shouldn't necessarily make someone weaker This could make the system much more interesting. A sin might offer immediate spiritual power at a cost. Pride: enormous confidence, resistance to intimidation → eventually incapable of accepting help. Greed: ability to bind things to yourself → eventually everything becomes something to possess. Envy: copy or steal qualities from others → gradually lose your own identity. Wrath: explosive spiritual power → increasingly difficult to control. Sloth: shut out pain, responsibility, even spiritual attacks → eventually become numb to everything. Meanwhile, virtues tend to be harder and slower, but create lasting power. You could make the spirit visually change A person's soul/spirit might actually reveal what is dominating them. Someone doesn't become "a Wrath person" because they got angry once. Their spirit changes because they repeatedly choose wrath when given the opportunity to choose patience. That gives you a nice progression: temptation → choice → habit → spiritual change → character And importantly, characters could move backward and forward. A prideful character could slowly develop humility. Someone known for charity could fall into greed. One particularly interesting rule I would make Pride/Humility the central pair. Pride could amplify the other sins: "I deserve this." → Greed "I deserve pleasure." → Gluttony/Lust "I deserve what they have." → Envy "How dare they do this to me?" → Wrath "This responsibility is beneath me." → Sloth Humility does the opposite: it allows someone to actually see themselves truthfully, which makes developing the other virtues possible. That could give your story's spiritual system a surprisingly deep structure: the seven aren't seven unrelated powers—they're seven ways a spirit can become ordered or disordered.
