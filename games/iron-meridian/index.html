Dice Battles
  • Everyone gives orders at once, then Submit. Buy units, attach supports, send battalions, and pick a combat order in each battle.
  • HP are platoons. Each company rolls a die. Organization (ORG) is how together a battalion is — at 0 it retreats.
  • Fighter coverage only protects from air attacks, never from a ground fight.
  • Every icon and number is labeled. Tap it for a short explanation, or open the Guide (top right) any time.
How a turn works (everyone at once)
  • Buy units with production points (PP) and order your moves. Nobody sees anyone else’s orders.
  • Move: all orders run together. Armies that end up in the same territory as an enemy start a battle.
  • Battle: every battle rolls the dice once per turn. Each unit hits on its attack value (or defense value, if it holds the land) or lower. Battles carry on turn after turn until one side is gone or pulls out.
  • Deploy new units at a factory, then collect income. A nation without its capital earns nothing.
  • Infrastructure replaces buying units: every switched-on infrastructure line builds one unit of its type in its territory each turn while your PP last (cheapest work first, in map order). Switch lines off to save PP, buy new lines, and capture the enemy’s.
  • Officers fight with the units around them. A major raises the number 3 units hit on by 1 (an attack of 1 becomes 2). A colonel adds 1 more for the units of up to 3 majors, and a general 1 more for the units of up to 3 colonels.
  • Air war comes before each ground battle. Fighters only fight planes. AA guns fire up to 3 shots each at enemy planes and hit on a 1. Bombers that get through hit either the troops or the infrastructure there, then fly home; they never join the ground battle.
  • Up close: zoom in past 200% (the + button, Ctrl + scroll or the + key) and the unit counts turn into the units themselves, one figure per unit. Tap a row of figures to pick those units up, just like a unit icon.
  • Four kinds of infrastructure: infantry, artillery (artillery or AA guns), armor (light, medium or heavy) and plane (fighters, bombers or recon planes).
  • Five kinds of infrastructure: infantry, artillery (artillery or AA guns), armor (light, medium or heavy), plane (fighters, bombers or recon planes) and officer (majors). In a territory’s window, pick what each kind builds there. Switching costs 75% of a new line’s price for each line switched, and while a switch is in progress nothing in that territory is built that turn. A territory holds only as many lines as its income value, and every nation starts with only a few lines.
  • Officers in this game can’t be bought. Officer infrastructure lines build majors. Promote a major to colonel (3 PP) or a colonel to general (4 PP) in the window of the territory where it stands.
  • Handling units: click a unit to pick up all of that kind, click again to drop one, right-click to put them down. Units that already have orders sit on yellow dashes; click them to change the order, right-click to cancel it. Click an arrow to change how many units it carries. The mouse wheel zooms. Battles are striped in the attacker’s colour and each side is labelled, and units lost last turn stay on the map, greyed out, for one turn.
  • Fog of war: you only see enemy units next to your own land and troops. Recon planes see 2 territories around them, and a recon flight shows you its target for the next turn.
  • At sea: ships stay in sea zones and are built at a port (a factory on the coast). A transport that stays put lets 2 land units a turn cross its sea zone to any shore it touches, unless enemy warships are there. Fighters fly over water but can’t stop on it.
